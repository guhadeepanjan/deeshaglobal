NASA scientist: farm fires may be seven times the official count

Satellite research by Dr Hiren Jethva of Morgan State University and NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center estimates about 34,000 farm fires in Punjab and Haryana between 1 October and 9 November - against 4,459 incidents recorded by the Commission for Air Quality Management. Farmers have gradually shifted burning to hours the government satellites do not observe, the research suggests.

Photo: Paddy field (scene photo) - Wikimedia CommonsRead at NDTV ↗

NASA will turn Mars rover testbeds into a nuclear-powered Moon rover

NASA is pressing ahead with PROMISE - the Polar Rover for Observation, Mapping, and In-Situ Exploration - a lunar rover repurposed from the Perseverance and Curiosity engineering testbeds, for under $400 million. Powered by a radioisotope generator left over from the Cassini era, it is due to land near the lunar south pole in late 2028 for an 18-month mission, delivered by Blue Origin under a $94 million base contract.

Photo: The Moon - Wikimedia CommonsRead at The Register ↗

L&T delivers the fourth crew-escape shroud set for Gaganyaan-2

L&T Precision Engineering and Systems has delivered the fourth set of the Crew Escape Ogive Shroud (CEOS) for the Gaganyaan-2 mission to ISRO from its Coimbatore facility. The shroud is part of the Crew Escape System, the safety mechanism designed to pull astronauts away from the launch vehicle in an emergency during lift-off or ascent.

Photo: Space station (scene photo) - Wikimedia CommonsRead at The Times of India ↗

NASA picks Viasat to design the commercial relay that succeeds TDRS

NASA has selected Viasat for Phase 1 of its NEXUS programme to design a commercial Ka-band relay service for spacecraft still dependent on the ageing TDRS network, under a contract with a maximum potential value of $460.9 million. The design phase runs to 3 May 2027, and NASA wants a service compatible with existing users for at least 15 years. The ISS and Hubble are among TDRS's remaining users.

Photo: Communications satellite (scene photo) - Wikimedia CommonsRead at SatNews ↗

Starfish Space loses contact with its Otter 24C debris-test satellite

Seattle startup Starfish Space lost contact with its Otter 24C satellite on 3 November, about a month after launch, ending its NASA technology demonstration for in-orbit servicing and debris work. A fault-protection glitch on the Astro Digital-supplied satellite bus switched off the radio. The company says its earlier Otter Pup 2 prototype recently closed to within 25 metres of another satellite.
